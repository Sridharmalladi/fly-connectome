How these slices were made

Each compact slice preserves one shortest directed path returned by a recorded, bounded-length Cypher query against the public Male CNS v1.0 NeuPrint API. These source-derived rows are real measurements; the slice is deliberately not a complete neighborhood or a fresh search for alternative paths. The annotation includes neuron IDs, published cell types, superclass, class, instance and the recorded ROI with the greatest synapse weight. See the complete slice manifest and query provenance.

02 THE ANALYSIS, EXPLAINED

A simple method.
Made inspectable.

Each choice is visible and repeatable. The analysis starts with descriptive statistics, then treats the connections as a directed, weighted graph.

01

Check coverage first

Count unique neurons, directed connections, annotated nodes and their cell types. The coverage rate uses graph neurons as its denominator.

02

Respect edge direction

A connection runs from the presynaptic sender to the postsynaptic receiver. Duplicate directed pairs are summed; self-connections and invalid rows are excluded.

03

Compare two questions

Shortest: breadth-first search minimizes connection count. Strength-weighted: Dijkstra minimizes the sum of 1 / log(1 + synapse count), balancing stronger edges against extra steps.

04

Test the assumption

Raise the minimum edge threshold and rerun. If candidate paths change, that sensitivity is part of the result—not something to hide.

03 WHAT THE SCIENCE CAN SAY

A map of connections
is not a movie of a mind.

The Male CNS v1.0 is a publicly released, expert-reconstructed connectome of an adult male fruit fly. Its wiring is a remarkable map—but a map alone cannot show which neurons fired, when they fired, or what the animal experienced.

Synapse counts are structural measurements, not direct measures of signal strength, behavioral importance or causation. Missing annotations do not imply a neuron has no role. Results from one specimen are not population-level evidence.
